MoonRobo product requirements
OPC OS portfolio  |  30 September 2026  |  Version 1 for product review
MoonRobo is an experimental robotics safety and bridge pack for named operators qualifying a bounded action on a specific robot. Its baseline defines robot readiness, a simulation-only design path, typed adapter/reconciliation contracts and a cockpit for queue, telemetry and proof inspection. The local platform queue passed 9/9; no device, SDK E1 or physical bridge was run. Physical release remains conditional on named hardware, firmware, environment, operator and negative-path evidence.
Repository maturity claim experimental bounded hardware validation
This document defines the product baseline and a separately proposed roadmap. Acceptance tests below are release criteria unless a cited observation explicitly says they passed. Current source coverage does not establish production deployment or customer value.
Product philosophy and scope
Physical work requires an authority chain that survives uncertainty. A model intention, digital design validation or lunar simulation result can inform a proposal but cannot establish current hardware readiness. The robot product owns command and interlock policy. Before dispatch, a named operator must see the bounded action, current calibration and safety evidence, and a valid separate grant. Human stop and takeover remain available.
For a robotics service founder, the company commitment is a clearly scoped service or trial with inspected proof. The operational chain should connect approved work to the exact robot, command attempt, telemetry and reviewed outcome. A completed queue item is not physical success, customer acceptance or received payment. The first defensible product is one qualified hardware profile and bounded trial; most founders should never need this specialist surface.

User scenarios
Robot profile calibration readiness and safety gates
MR-W01 | Baseline scope. An operator prepares a robot for a named task
1. Resolve the robot identity and versioned profile
2. Inspect calibration, health and environment evidence
3. Evaluate task-specific readiness and interlocks
4. Present blockers or a bounded action for separate authorization
Edge case A valid profile can coexist with stale calibration; no single green status should erase task-specific limitations.
Failure and recovery Missing readiness evidence leaves the action blocked while inspection and diagnostics remain available.
Acceptance The operator can identify which evidence is current, who owns the gate and why an action is refused.
Evidence references README.mbt.md#Product Boundary; docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Ownership
Simulation only design build and validate
MR-W02 | Baseline scope. A designer proposes an excavation-haul rover concept
1. Submit the supported typed digital design request
2. Build robot profile and URDF artifacts
3. Validate structural consistency and record the design receipt
4. Export exact design identity for a digital mission study
Edge case Structural digital readiness is neither mission feasibility nor tested hardware readiness.
Failure and recovery Invalid geometry, mismatched artifacts or unsupported design inputs prevent a valid design receipt.
Acceptance The package binds profile, URDF and validation identities and remains explicitly simulation-only.
Evidence references README.mbt.md#Design-linked digital build
MoonFlow adapter and bridge reconciliation
MR-W03 | Baseline scope. An authorized typed operation reaches the pack or an attempt is interrupted
1. Validate operation, target and authority envelope
2. Create an attempt with stable identity
3. Dispatch only when the named bridge and current gates qualify
4. Reconcile receipt or telemetry before accepting completion or retry
Edge case A lost acknowledgement is an unknown outcome, not a failed action that is automatically safe to repeat.
Failure and recovery An unavailable SDK E1 or physical bridge leaves physical dispatch blocked; local digital queue behavior does not fill the gap.
Acceptance The operation record shows requested, dispatched, unknown and reconciled states distinctly; physical acceptance remains to be tested.
Evidence references docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Capability status; docs/BRIDGE_PROTOCOL.md
Cockpit telemetry and proof sessions
MR-W04 | Baseline scope. An operator monitors a bounded session
1. Inspect queue, readiness and current authority
2. Observe timestamped telemetry and proof artifacts
3. Exercise human stop or takeover through the qualified platform path
4. Review incidents and separate observed outcome from requested intent
Edge case Telemetry loss can prevent proof of completion even if a command was sent; an empty queue is not proof of safety.
Failure and recovery A stale stream or unexplained command outcome triggers a held or incident state pending platform-specific response.
Acceptance The reviewer can reconstruct command intent, observed execution, interventions and remaining unknowns.
Evidence references docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Users and jobs; README.mbt.md
Digital to physical trial gate
MR-W05 | Roadmap scope. A MoonMoon simulation is offered as evidence for a trial
1. Bind the exact simulation and robot design receipts
2. Check current hardware, calibration, environment and safety policy
3. Show the proposed bounded action and named authorization
4. Dispatch only through a qualified bridge or record refusal
Edge case A successful simulation may be irrelevant to changed hardware or an unsafe environment; refusal is a valid outcome.
Failure and recovery Missing matching evidence or expired authority blocks dispatch and preserves the proposal for correction.
Acceptance The reviewer sees separate digital result, current readiness and physical grant before any device action.
Evidence references README.mbt.md#Product Boundary; docs/BRIDGE_PROTOCOL.md
Incident and proof reconciliation
MR-W06 | Roadmap scope. A device result is unknown, failed or aborted
1. Collect raw telemetry and command acknowledgements
2. Reconcile what physically occurred before considering repetition
3. Record the incident, human interventions and corrective decision
4. Send reviewed outcome evidence to knowledge with exact session identity
Edge case Insufficient telemetry can leave an outcome unresolved; learning must preserve that uncertainty.
Failure and recovery Failed reconciliation prohibits automatic retry; the responsible human chooses the next safe action under platform policy.
Acceptance An interrupted action cannot be repeated merely because its queue receipt is missing, and incident lessons require named review.
Evidence references docs/BRIDGE_PROTOCOL.md; docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Ownership

Alternatives and optimality
Manufacturer cockpit and supervised checklist
Advantage Uses established platform controls with less integration
Cost Manual evidence and company-work linkage
When to choose Prefer for occasional trials or when manufacturer safety tooling is already sufficient
Robot middleware with custom safety integration
Advantage Flexible engineering control and wider ecosystem
Cost Significant integration and assurance work
When to choose Prefer when required robot capabilities exceed the narrow supported envelope
Narrow MoonRobo bridge
Advantage Shared authority, attempt and proof records for repeated bounded work
Cost Needs rigorous per-platform qualification
When to choose Use only when that continuity reduces real operator ambiguity or reconciliation burden
A credible first robotics PRD centers on one named platform and one bounded supervised action. Readiness, stop behavior and unknown-outcome reconciliation then become specific enough to test. Universal autonomy would spread assurance over untested hardware and environments.
The local 9/9 queue suite cannot show that a device stops, telemetry reflects physical state or a lost acknowledgement is safe to retry. Physical proof and negative-path qualification therefore remain explicit release work.
The digital design path can support MoonMoon studies before physical bridge qualification. Its output remains structurally validated simulation evidence. Later physical integration must never convert old digital artifacts into current hardware readiness.
A manufacturer cockpit and disciplined checklist may be the safer, cheaper option for a small number of trials. MoonRobo is justified where repeated work needs shared identity, bounded authority and recoverable proof. Its success should be judged by reduced ambiguity and trustworthy refusal as much as successful execution, with no claim of absolute optimality.
