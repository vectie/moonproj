MoonPoly product requirements
OPC OS portfolio  |  30 September 2026  |  Version 1 for product review
MoonPoly is a policy-formation and advisory-delivery pack. Its baseline supports policy understanding, option appraisal, editable public-document analysis, cutoff-safe forecasting and bounded writing review. It preserves dissent, provenance and procedural readiness while a named public body retains adoption, amendment and publication authority. The seeded Changping 2021–2027 slice and three passing formation tests do not establish other-jurisdiction readiness or a live policy campaign.
Repository maturity claim first packed Changping slice with many local contracts
This document defines the product baseline and a separately proposed roadmap. Acceptance tests below are release criteria unless a cited observation explicitly says they passed. Current source coverage does not establish production deployment or customer value.
Product philosophy and scope
Policy analysis should make the path from official text and evidence to interpretation and alternatives visible. A resident asking what a policy means, an analyst appraising options and an authorized official deciding what to adopt have different jobs. The product should preserve these three modes rather than presenting a draft, a readiness score or a generated forecast as an official act.
For an independent policy consultant or founder affected by regulation, the useful company loop is a sourced question, a bounded advisory commitment, an inspected brief and separate client review. Knowledge retention can support future work, but acceptance of an imported source does not certify its truth. Policy adoption belongs outside the company operating system, and customer payment is not inferred from completion of an advisory document.

2. Navigate typed stage and domain views with governance layers
3. Inspect actors, resources and evidence drill-down
4. Separate the official source from reconstructed interpretation

Design and appraise policy options

1. Document problem, affected stakeholders and evidence

Cutoff safe document forecast
MP-W04 | Baseline scope. An analyst asks which policy document or option could emerge after a cutoff
1. Freeze available evidence and cutoff
2. Rank alternatives with stated assumptions
3. Expose invalidation conditions and missing evidence
4. Label the output as an analytical forecast
Edge case Support scores are not calibrated probabilities and cannot establish government intent.
Failure and recovery Evidence published after the cutoff is rejected from the original forecast and requires a separately versioned update.
Acceptance A retrospective evaluation reproduces the original evidence set without hindsight leakage.

Founder policy impact brief
MP-W06 | Roadmap scope. A founder asks how a dated change affects one customer segment

The best OPC entry point is likely a founder impact brief or a consulting deliverable, not a dense formation matrix. The matrix can supply traceability behind those views. It earns its cost only when a reviewer needs to traverse policy history, governance layers or contested assumptions.
The source boundary is strong: public authority owns legal acts, and support scores remain analytical. The PRD should preserve that distinction throughout labels, exports and client review. A local draft that looks official can mislead even if the backend is carefully bounded.
A narrower first product should choose one jurisdiction and one recurring advisory question, then validate citation fidelity and client comprehension. The Changping example and formation tests are useful development evidence, but generalizing their success to a live jurisdiction or multi-role campaign would overstate readiness.
There is no absolute optimum between typed policy infrastructure and a careful human memo. If a source document and checklist answer the client question reliably, the simpler method wins. Expansion should follow evidence of reduced reconstruction effort, fewer missed procedural gaps and repeatable client review.
