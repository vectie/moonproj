MoonGate product requirements
OPC OS portfolio  |  30 September 2026  |  Version 1 for product review
MoonGate is a local model access boundary for route operators, application integrators and usage reviewers. Its baseline combines compatible proxy routing, host-held credentials, request inspection, controlled routing policy and exact capability resolution. The repository describes a feature-testing alpha. Four native routing-selection tests passed, but no current provider request, billing reconciliation or operator interface journey was exercised. The next product milestone should qualify one usable route and its failure behavior before expanding provider breadth. Task-level spending envelopes and a cross-product readiness view are separate roadmap work.
Repository maturity claim feature-testing alpha
This document defines the product baseline and a separately proposed roadmap. Acceptance tests below are release criteria unless a cited observation explicitly says they passed. Current source coverage does not establish production deployment or customer value.
Product philosophy and scope
A client should know what it can call, under whose authority, and with what observable cost without obtaining provider credentials. MoonGate owns model access and provider/model selection. It does not decide the company objective, grant arbitrary tool authority or declare a generated answer correct. A route advertised in an inventory is a possibility; an accepted exact resolution and valid execution authority are prerequisites for dispatch.
Within the OPC operating sequence, an approved commitment may consume a model through MoonGate and return a usage receipt for later review. The request result is an intermediate artifact: another product or person inspects its quality, records customer acceptance and reconciles actual cash separately. MoonGate can improve accountability through bounded consumption and truthful blockers, but no present evidence establishes reduced founder coordination time or a completed commercial loop.
Users and jobs
Route operator
Configure compatible endpoints and host-secret references, inspect health and control routing changes.
Trigger A client needs a route or a provider becomes unreliable.
Successful outcome The selected route is explainable, recoverable and does not expose credentials.
Application integrator
Connect MoonClaw or another client to the local proxy and resolve a requested capability exactly.
Trigger An authorized job needs model access or an installed adapter.
Successful outcome Dispatch receives a usable contract or a specific blocker rather than a misleading ready flag.
Usage reviewer
Understand requests, quota, estimated price and policy effects.
Trigger Unexpected usage, an incident or a budget review.
Successful outcome A redacted evidence trail separates recorded consumption from missing telemetry and settled provider charges.

User scenarios
Configure and use a compatible local route
MG01 | Baseline scope. An operator has an authorized provider account and a client request.
1. Configure the endpoint and model with a host-secret reference; retain loopback binding by default.
2. Resolve an eligible provider/model, dispatch the compatible request and return its response or stream.
3. Record route choice, completion or failure, and available usage without exposing the secret.
Edge case A configured provider may be unavailable; streamed output may already have reached the client when an upstream fails.
Failure and recovery Fail closed when credentials or eligibility are missing. Apply only permitted failover; do not silently restart a partially delivered stream. A retry must be visible as another attempt.
Acceptance A fixture client receives supported responses, missing configuration blocks dispatch, and disconnect/failover tests show no hidden duplicate completion. Real-provider qualification remains outstanding.
Evidence references README.mbt.md; docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Capability status
Inspect usage quota pricing and requests
MG02 | Baseline scope. A reviewer investigates cost or request behavior.
1. Select the relevant request interval and inspect recorded usage, quota, pricing availability and latency percentiles.
2. Open redacted request detail and distinguish measured values, estimates and unsupported fields.
3. Use the evidence to decide whether a route or policy needs review.
Edge case A provider may omit usage or price data; an empty interval has no meaningful percentile.
Failure and recovery Show unavailable fields rather than zero spend or fabricated metrics. Refresh or reprocess available telemetry without creating a second request charge record.
Acceptance Known fixtures reconcile to the visible totals, missing values stay unknown and sensitive payload fields remain redacted after filtering and export.
Evidence references docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Capability status
Review activate and roll back a routing policy
MG03 | Baseline scope. An operator proposes different provider/model selection.
1. Evaluate the candidate in shadow mode and review its selection effects.
2. Obtain fresh health and a named actor decision bound to MoonLib principal and authority references.
3. Activate with a durable receipt; if necessary restore an identified prior policy through a separately authorized rollback.
Edge case Health can become stale between review and activation; a policy must not change non-routing authority.
Failure and recovery Reject stale or unauthorized activation without changing the active policy. Recover an uncertain result by reading its receipt before submitting another change.
Acceptance Shadow evaluation does not alter production selection; activation and rollback each leave actor-bound evidence and unauthorized attempts have no effect.
Evidence references docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Security and data
Resolve an installed capability for dispatch
MG04 | Baseline scope. MoonFlow or MoonDesk needs a specific capability.
1. Discover candidate products or packs without treating the inventory as a grant.
2. Match the requested capability to an installed pack, compatible adapter and current health.
3. Return the accepted exact resolution for the caller to use with its own execution authority.
Edge case Two similarly named entries or an installed but unhealthy adapter must not resolve by convenient substitution.
Failure and recovery Return the mismatched or missing prerequisite and prevent dispatch. After installation or health recovery, perform resolution again rather than reuse rejected evidence.
Acceptance Ambiguous, absent and unhealthy fixtures fail closed; the successful fixture identifies the exact executable contract.
Evidence references docs/CAPABILITY_REGISTRY.md; docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Verification
Check endpoint syntax and benchmark honestly
MG05 | Baseline scope. An operator checks configuration before requesting live execution.
1. Run URL syntax validation with a result explicitly limited to syntax.
2. Run a deterministic fixture benchmark and label its data source.
3. Attempt live benchmarking only when a qualified verifier, adapter and required authority exist.
Edge case A syntactically valid URL can be unreachable or incompatible.
Failure and recovery Missing live prerequisites block the benchmark; repair configuration and retry the appropriate check without upgrading prior fixture results into live evidence.
Acceptance Tests prove syntax checking makes no network probe and fixture scores never render as provider availability or live performance.
Evidence references docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md#Security and data
Enforce a commitment spending envelope
MG06 | Roadmap scope. A founder approves model spending for one MoonProj task.
1. Receive a non-secret task reference, currency, ceiling, scope and approval reference.
2. Reserve estimated request exposure before dispatch and enforce the remaining envelope across retries.
3. Return usage receipts to the commitment ledger for review and later charge reconciliation.
Edge case Streaming usage, price uncertainty and simultaneous requests can exceed a naive check-after-completion budget.
Failure and recovery Block when safe exposure cannot be bounded. Preserve unresolved reservations after uncertain responses, reconcile them, then release or adjust with evidence.
Acceptance Concurrent and retry fixtures cannot double-spend the same envelope; missing prices block or require an explicitly approved alternative bound.
Expose honest route readiness before work
MG07 | Roadmap scope. An application prepares to start delegated work.
1. Request a readiness snapshot for the exact configured model and capability.
2. Show configuration, authority, live-health age and any missing prerequisite separately.
3. Revalidate dispatch-sensitive prerequisites when work actually starts.
Edge case Readiness can expire while an operator views the screen.
Failure and recovery Report the exact blocker and a safe next action. A refresh replaces stale evidence but does not grant missing authority.
Acceptance A syntax-only endpoint, stale health and unauthorized route each remain blocked; an eligible live route displays the model actually selected.

Interfaces and handoffs
MoonClaw and compatible clients
Payload Model request, response and non-secret request identity
Contract Client holds task authority; MoonGate owns only model routing and credential isolation.
Failure behavior Return actionable route failure and attempt identity; caller decides whether work may resume.
MoonFlow and MoonDesk
Payload Inventory and accepted exact capability resolution
Contract A product listing is never executable permission.
Failure behavior Missing adapter or stale health blocks dispatch until resolution is repeated.
MoonLib
Payload Principal and authority references bound to policy receipts
Contract Activation must match the named actor and approved scope.
Failure behavior Unverifiable authority leaves the active policy unchanged.
LunaNexa or another provider
Payload Provider-neutral model request and usage response
Contract LunaNexa is optional behind generic routing, not a source-level suite dependency.
Failure behavior Provider unavailability follows declared retry and failover rules; no implied paid entitlement.

Alternatives and optimality
Direct provider client
Advantage Fewer moving parts for one trusted application.
Cost Application owns credentials, telemetry and provider differences.
When to choose Prefer when one route and minimal governance suffice.
Existing managed gateway
Advantage May offer qualified multi-user operations and support.
Cost Additional service dependency and possible data-routing constraints.
When to choose Evaluate when organizational credential control exceeds this local alpha.
MoonGate local boundary
Advantage Shared local routing and evidence without embedding provider secrets in clients.
Cost Operator must qualify adapters, releases and recovery.
When to choose Choose where several local clients benefit from this boundary.
The strongest design center is a narrow, inspectable local model boundary. It concentrates credential handling and routing evidence where they can be reviewed, while leaving task purpose and customer decisions with their owners. This is a defensible fit for multiple local clients, not a universal best architecture.
The current PRD should be narrowed around one demonstrably usable client/provider route. Adding providers before qualifying cancellation, stream failure and missing telemetry would increase the number of advertised paths faster than trustworthy coverage. Signed release and configuration recovery deserve qualification before wider deployment claims.
Readiness should describe distinct facts instead of collapsing configuration, health and authority into one reassuring label. More checks can delay startup and health expires quickly; therefore snapshots need explicit freshness and dispatch revalidation rather than a promise of future success.
Commitment budgets improve accountability only if uncertain cost is handled conservatively. Reservations may reject useful work when pricing is incomplete, but charging after completion cannot reliably enforce a ceiling. Keep this opt-in roadmap contract small and external to the company ledger. A direct provider client remains better when these governance benefits do not justify an extra service.
Open product decisions
Decision Which client and provider establish the first qualified route?
Recommendation Select one named pair with streaming and cancellation needs.
Consequence Support claims remain bounded to measured compatibility.
Decision What telemetry retention and payload inspection are acceptable?
Recommendation Choose a short, explicit retention window and redaction-first inspection.
Consequence Some debugging detail is sacrificed to reduce sensitive content exposure.
Decision How should unknown request cost affect a budget?
Recommendation Require a conservative approved exposure bound or block dispatch.
Consequence Some providers cannot participate in a hard spending envelope.
